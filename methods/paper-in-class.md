# Paper and pencil, in class

## How it works
The assessment is moved inside the room. There is no asynchronous authoring window, so there is no separate authoring record to compare against. The artifact and the act of creation are the same event.

## What it sees
Everything, and therefore nothing useful: the handwriting, the ink, the pacing, the visible struggle. All of it is inline and self-authenticating. There is no diff to run.

## Defeated by
Nothing.

## Fails when
Time pressure — a single period is often not enough for a real essay, which is why teachers split it across days ("turn it in each day, do it in sections, 2-3 days"). Also fails on legibility, on accommodations, and on physical conditions where handwriting is genuinely inaccessible.

**The legibility hazard is not a joke and it runs one direction.** This venue removes every text-level countermeasure, so the only remaining variable is *handwriting*, and nobody grades what they can't read. An operator who has typed for three years has degraded handwriting speed and legibility, and no amount of prose quality compensates — the constraint stops being rhetorical and becomes physiographic mid-exam. The failure mode is not "flagged as AI," it's "marked down and told to type next time." Practice handwriting under time pressure before the first blue-book, not during it. This is the one place in the whole taxonomy where the correct preparation is physical.

## Notes
The single most repeated answer across every r/Teachers thread on this topic. Not a detection improvement — an architectural one. Teachers are not getting better at detection; they are removing the attack surface by removing the attack window.

**No text-level or process-level countermeasure exists.** Any skill claiming otherwise is selling something. See SKILL.md §1.2 (surface vs. vector): surface is enormous, vector is zero.
