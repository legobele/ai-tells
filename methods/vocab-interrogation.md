# Vocabulary interrogation

## How it works
A narrow Special Case of `oral-defense.md`, filed separately because it's the most frequently deployed version and has a distinct failure profile.

Pick two or three words from the submission that the student is unlikely to have produced, and ask what they mean. Or ask them to use one in a new sentence. Or ask why that word rather than its common synonym.

> "Choose a couple non-common words from the essay and ask them what they mean."
> "Pick a few vocab words that seem unlikely to have come from the student and ask about them."

## What it sees
The difference between *recognizing* a word and *owning* it. Productive versus receptive vocabulary. A student can read a word in their own essay and still not be able to define it, because they didn't write it.

Near-zero false positive rate: someone who wrote a word almost always knows it.

## Defeated by
Knowing the words. That's it.

The countermeasure is the same as the tell-avoidance rule in SKILL.md §8: **write only at the vocabulary band you can defend under questioning.** Every escaped word is a live vulnerability.

## Fails when
- The instructor picks a word the student plausibly knows but can't articulate under pressure — anxiety, not ignorance. Good practitioners accept a paraphrase or a usage example rather than demanding a dictionary definition.
- Multilingual learners get hit by this disproportionately, since the gap between receptive and productive vocabulary is wider and more normal for them.
- The instructor picks too common a word, which proves nothing.

## Notes
The single highest-fidelity, lowest-cost control in the entire directory. One question, no tooling, near-zero error rate.

Also the control that makes `ai-detectors.md` irrelevant: any teacher using this doesn't need a percentage.

**Language-class amplification:** in a foreign-language course this control is native to the curriculum and considerably stronger, because the instructor already knows the student's productive vocabulary from speech. See SKILL.md §9.
